# ATLAS 2.0 independent evaluation

Baseline reference: `4f760bb0b9ee6dc017c9ed2c57a705be5c4891ec`. All historical benchmark artifacts and controller hashes remain unchanged. A fresh Linux reproduction of original selected seed 5001 returned exactly **21.177817772778404 s**, 870/889 completed trips, matching published per-seed physics. Wall time and execution latency vary with machine load.

The new protocol is `atlas-2-protocol.json`. Native initial smoke runs are diagnostics, not final evidence. The final frozen prototype uses Linux SUMO 1.27.1, paired policy-independent resolved routes, no teleport, common source states, equal horizons and reset state. Published RESCO demand evaluates Cologne1 and Ingolstadt1 on 20 new seeds 34001–34020 each, 1,800 seconds. These comparisons refer to the **previous frozen adaptive MPC**, not the much older failed cyclic ATLAS algorithm.

Five-city OSM runs use separate assumed routes and short 300-second horizons. Two smoke seeds 17001–17002 precede a new nominal 20-seed replication (20001–20020) without policy changes. Independent OSM/map validation, real demand calibration and additional demand regimes are still required. Seed changes randomize SUMO dynamics under the same pinned OD/routes template; they do not represent twenty independent measured rush hours.

Mean delay uses the unchanged `trip_metrics` definition across all scheduled vehicles, including departure delay and available unfinished time loss. Completed-only travel time is separately labeled. An unfinished trip is not extrapolated to its future full delay, which is a limitation. Queue metrics sum **controlled inbound lanes**, not every road in the map. Stops, completed throughput and worst observed approach delay remain visible. SUMO CO₂/fuel rates are model estimates integrated over 1-second steps, not measured field emissions; fuel rates use mg/s, with total kg conversion. Person-weighted delay, pedestrian wait and bus delay have no observed inputs in these car-only runs.

Report sample SD, mean 95% t intervals, paired per-seed differences and bootstrap intervals; lower delay is better, higher completed throughput is better. Means of paired percent reductions differ from ratios of aggregate means. Confidence intervals from two smoke seeds are unstable. Negative findings remain visible. Raw completed run artifacts, traces and settings are retained; compressed publication files are lossless, not scratch.

One-seed Austin outage/slowdown and ablation runs are sensitivity checks, not statistical acceptance. Lane slowdown sets one approach to 0.1 m/s in the middle third and restores its original speed. Source outage removes state observations in the middle third. The no-topology-fallback variant can improve this Austin case; it is not used to reselect the final controller.

The independent METR-LA probabilistic extension evaluates persistence/EWMA at 5/15/30 minutes, with chronological test origins, causal filling, only already-resolved calibration residuals, invalid-target/gap exclusion and finite-sample interval ranks. Historic boosted model results stay separate. A future-suffix perturbation test verifies that earlier forecasts and interval widths cannot consume future targets. Temporal dependence means empirical coverage must be reported without exchangeability guarantees.
