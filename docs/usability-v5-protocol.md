# ATLAS 5 usability study protocol

This is a study design, not a completed human evaluation. **Participants: 0.**
Automated browser and accessibility checks measure engineering behavior only.

Recruit 5–8 consenting adults unfamiliar with ATLAS, including at least two
transportation practitioners if available. Participation and recording require
explicit consent; no agency account or operational traffic permissions are implied.
Use the public precomputed demo, the same browser, and a clean storage context.
Record browser/device, prior domain knowledge and assistive technology use. Avoid
coaching; request concurrent narration only after the first unaided attempt.

| Task | Completion criterion | Time limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Find a camera in the assigned city | Identify its source and whether imagery is live or cached | 2 min |
| Open a supported corridor | Select the assigned city and inspect imported geometry | 2 min |
| Assess calibration | Correctly distinguish observed counts, estimated demand and unavailable simulated-state accuracy | 3 min |
| Replay an experiment | Open ATLAS 5, choose high demand, compare max-pressure and play linked timelines | 3 min |
| Interpret results | Explain percentage direction, paired interval and why failed promotion prevents a production claim | 3 min |
| Export a pilot assessment | Download the correct city/case with final seeds and identify unvalidated economic assumptions | 2 min |

Counterbalance city and task order where tasks do not depend on previous steps.
Use Seattle high demand and Toronto low demand in balanced interpretation tasks
so participants encounter both a favorable result and a regression. Define success
before recruitment: complete unaided within the limit and satisfy the criterion.
Record failed attempts, navigation errors, moderator interventions and task time;
retain incomplete tasks in the denominator. Report median time and range, raw
success counts, qualitative confusion and participant context. With this small
sample, avoid population significance or commercial-readiness claims.

After each task collect a 1–7 ease rating; at the end collect a standard SUS survey
only if the complete instrument and scoring are followed. Store anonymized results
outside source control and publish aggregate findings with consent. A subsequent
revision requires a separate follow-up cohort; do not reuse participants as an
independent comparison. No study has been run because no participants were provided.
